I audited 88,916 WinGo 30S rounds across 31 calendar dates—and the most useful result was an evidence-based limit on prediction claims.

One saved 1,000-feature model scored:
• 8.76% for the exact digit
• 48.26% for Big/Small
• 49.52% for primary Red/Green

Those trained-model results did not establish a reliable predictive advantage. A recent-history Big/Small baseline did reach 51.68% in an exploratory backtest; I have retained that result with its limitations rather than presenting a selective story.

The publication also reconciles two dataset versions: 51 added rounds, 49 completed premium fields, and no changes to previously recorded winning digits.

Another important correction: a report organized into “110 dimensions” is not automatically 110 validated cryptographic tests. The repository documents where earlier verdicts were hardcoded, methods were unsuitable, or conclusions went beyond the evidence.

I’m sharing the whitepaper, dataset release files, saved evaluation evidence, scripts, checksums and an offline reproduction workflow. No new training was performed for this publication, and no seed was recovered.

My takeaway: statistical regularity, predictive accuracy and proof of a deployed generator are three different claims. Credible research keeps them separate.
